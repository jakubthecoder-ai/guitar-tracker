# Guitar skill taxonomies, curricula and placement (rock / metal / blues / prog metal, electric)

Research note: several primary sources blocked automated fetching (HTTP 403 on RSL/AMEB syllabus PDFs and the justinguitar.com site map), so some details come from search-result snippets of those documents or from secondary pages. Forum-sourced numbers are flagged as anecdotal. Items under "Inferences" are design proposals built on the cited findings, not sourced facts.

## 1. Rockschool/RSL electric guitar grades Debut–8: technique features and tempo benchmarks

### Takeaway
RSL grades combine an approximate "time learning" descriptor with a fixed exam structure: pieces, technical exercises, sight reading or improvisation, ear tests and musicianship questions. The tempo of technical exercises rises slowly, from about 70 bpm (Debut/G1) through 80 (G2), 90 (G3) and 80 (G5 scales, 8ths per one source) to 100 bpm (G6–8). Expressive and lead techniques arrive around G2–G3: palm muting and alternate picking at G2, and bends, pre-bends, pedal tones and cross picking at G3. These tempos are low, so RSL grades work better as anchors for breadth (theory, reading, ear, chord vocabulary) than for speed.

### Cited Findings
- Exam weighting (Grade Exam): performance pieces 60%, technical exercises 15%, sight reading or improvisation 10%, ear tests 10%, musicianship questions 5%. A Graded Certificate is 75% pieces and 25% technical. A Performance Certificate is 5 pieces at 20% each. — [RSL: Learn Electric Guitar](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)
- **Debut:** for candidates learning "for a short time". Pieces up to 1 min with "simple position work". Scales are C major and A/E minor pentatonic at 70 bpm. Open chords are A, D, C, G, Am and Em. Includes a riff to a backing track and sight reading of a 4-bar rhythm on the low E with half and quarter notes. Ear tests are melodic recall (higher/lower) and rhythmic recall. — [RSL](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)
- **Grade 1:** for candidates learning "six months – one year". Pieces up to 1:15. Scales are C major, A natural minor, A/E minor pentatonic and G major pentatonic. Chords add power chords and open major/minor chords. Riff at 70 bpm. Sight reading in A minor, or improvisation in C major or A minor. Ear tests are 3-note melodic recall and rhythmic recall with quarters, eighths and quarter rests. — [RSL](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)
- **Grade 2:** "approximately one year to eighteen months". Book techniques are "palm muting, alternate picking, arpeggiated chords, new scales". Scales at 80 bpm: C/G major, A/E natural minor, and C/G minor and major pentatonic. Chords are 3-note power chords, F major, and m7 and maj7 chords. Improvisation in G major or E minor at 80–90 bpm. — [RSL](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)
- **Grade 3:** "approximately eighteen months to two years". Highlights "pedal tones, bends, arpeggios". Two-octave scales at 90 bpm in quarter notes: major, natural minor, minor and major pentatonic, and blues, in G, A and B. One-octave major/minor arpeggios at 90 bpm. Major and minor barre-chord sequences and dominant 7th chords. Riff at 90 bpm. Sight reading at 75–85 bpm. Improvisation at 80–90 bpm. Ear tests are melodic recall in G minor pentatonic at 85 bpm and rhythmic recall at 90 bpm. — [RSL](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)
- Grade 3 book content also lists "open-string pedal tones, bends, pre-bends, cross picking". — [RSL product page, Grade 3](https://www.rslawards.com/products/electric-guitar-grade-3/); [Shiksha listing](https://www.shiksha.com/online-courses/electric-guitar-grade-3-course-rsawl1)
- **Grade 5:** Group A technical exercises are major and minor scales at 80 bpm. — [MusicGurus RSL Grade 5 lesson](https://www.musicgurus.com/de/courses/rockschool-guitar-online-video-tutorial-grade-5/lessons/how-to-play-rockschool-technical-exercises-group-a-guitar-grade-5)
- **Grades 6–8:** Group A scales and modes are two octaves at 100 bpm. The Grade 8 set includes the whole-tone scale. Grades 6–8 add "Stylistic Studies" (e.g. metal through Latin) and a "Quick Study Piece" whose style follows the chosen stylistic study. — [RSL Technical Exercises 2018 PDF (AMEB)](https://rockschool.ameb.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Technical-Exercises-Electric-Guitar-2018-Online.pdf); [MusicGurus Grade 8](https://www.musicgurus.com/courses/rockschool-guitar-online-video-tutorial-grade-8/lessons/how-to-play-rockschool-technical-exercises-group-a-guitar-grade-8); [RSL Syllabus Guide 2018 (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/436121021/RSL-Guitar-Syllabus-Guide-2018-DIGITAL-31May2019-pdf)

### Inferences
- RSL time descriptors give a rough mapping for placement. Debut is under 6 months, G1 is 6–12 months, G2 is 12–18 months and G3 is 18–24 months. The higher grades probably follow the same pattern, but I could not verify G4–8 descriptors.
- RSL technical tempos are quarter- or eighth-note scale tempos that test accuracy, not speed. A coach should keep a separate speed axis (see section 3) and not use the RSL grade for it.
- RSL's five exam components fit directly onto a coach taxonomy: repertoire/performance, technique, reading/improvisation, ear and theory/knowledge.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the official technique lists for Grades 4–8, such as the grade where legato, tapping, sweep picking or vibrato are first assessed. The AMEB PDFs and the full RSL page were blocked or truncated. The [RSL Guitar Technical Handbook](https://www.rslawards.com/products/guitar-technical-handbook/) and the Syllabus Guide 2018 are the authoritative sources to check by hand.
- Tempo ranges for performance pieces per grade were not found.

## 2. Reference curricula and taxonomies (JustinGuitar, Troy Grady, Petrucci, Govan, blues, metal rhythm, prog)

### Takeaway
Each source covers a different dimension of the taxonomy. JustinGuitar uses a 9-grade "belt" system covering beginner to advanced. Troy Grady's Cracking the Code gives a mechanics taxonomy for picking: escape vs. trapped strokes, USX/DSX/DBX, and wrist/elbow/forearm joints. Petrucci's Rock Discipline gives a technique-category sequence. Blues courses converge on pentatonic positions, then bends and vibrato, then call and response with major/minor mixing and dynamics. Prog rhythm teaching starts with re-metering 4/4 riffs and grouping rhythms into cells of 2, 3, 4 and 5 sixteenths.

### Cited Findings
**JustinGuitar**
- The grading system has 9 grades, "colour-coded in the style of a karate belt system". — [JustinGuitar: grade system](https://justinguitar.com/guitar-lessons/what-s-the-justinguitar-grade-system-all-about-nws-9)
- The Beginner course has 3 grades, 23 modules, 204 lessons and 350+ songs. Grade 1 covers essential open chords and strumming. Grade 2 adds the F chord, rock and blues styles, and fingerpicking. Grade 3 prepares for intermediate playing. — [JustinGuitar Beginner Course](https://admin.justinguitar.com/beginner)
- The intermediate material (Grade 4+) is described by one community user as "very disjointed" with some duplication. This is opinion. — [JustinGuitar community](https://community.justinguitar.com/t/2021-intermediate-practice/2509)

**Troy Grady, Cracking the Code (picking mechanics)**
- Each pickstroke is either "escape" or "trapped". "In a downstroke escape or DSX motion, the downstroke is the escape stroke and the upstroke is the trapped stroke." USX is the inverse. — [troygrady.com DSX](https://troygrady.com/?p=133543)
- For efficient string changes, the last note on each string must be an escaped stroke. With DSX, "the final note on every string needs to be a downstroke." — [troygrady.com DSX](https://troygrady.com/?p=133543)
- Pickslant (how the pick looks, e.g. UWPS, zero-degree) is separate from escape (the path of the motion): "The appearance of the pick (pickslant) and the path of the picking motion (escape) are different things." — [troygrady.com DSX](https://troygrady.com/?p=133543)
- Motions are classified by joint: wrist (e.g. Andy Wood, Al Di Meola), elbow (Brendon Small, "textbook" elbow DSX) and wrist+forearm (Joscho Stephan, steep USX). Escape angle runs from shallow to steep. — [troygrady.com DSX](https://troygrady.com/?p=133543)
- A Cracking the Code contributor calls "two-way pickslanting" "sort of a deprecated concept". The Pickslanting Primer now teaches USX and DSX motions and switching between them. DBX (double escape) is a further category. Forum users note the lack of a basic glossary. — [Troy Grady forum](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/do-i-need-to-be-equally-as-good-with-dsx-usx-with-2wps/11218); [glossary thread](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/glossary-thread-dumb-question-or-3-or-4-or-more/63658); [mixed vs double escape](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/am-i-correct-in-understanding-that-mixed-escape-and-double-escape-are-different-or-is-that-wrong/83944)
- Diagnosis uses close-up ("Magnet" camera) and slow-motion video, because motions are hard to see at normal speed. — [troygrady.com DSX](https://troygrady.com/?p=133543)

**John Petrucci, Rock Discipline**
- Parts, from a community source: warm-ups (left hand, right hand, both hands), speed and accuracy, scale fragments and sequences, alternate-picked arpeggios, legato, sweep picking, chordal technique, and a final song. — [CustomsForge](https://customsforge.com/topic/10069-john-petruccis-rock-discipline-now-as-cdlc/)
- A retailer lists warm-ups, "developing speed and accuracy, chromatic exercises, playing with dynamics, connecting scale fragments to form long solo lines". The DVD runs about 120 minutes and the book has 83 pages. — [J.W. Pepper](https://www.jwpepper.com/rock-discipline-5980089/p); [Thomann](https://www.thomann.de/ie/alfred_music_publishing_john_petrucci_rock_discipline.htm)

**Guthrie Govan, Creative Guitar 1: Cutting Edge Techniques**
- Topics include pentatonic patterns, minor arpeggios, backing tracks and a thorough explanation of theory. Published by Sanctuary in 2002 with a CD. It is the first of a series of three. — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/852669); [GMC wiki](https://www.guitarmasterclass.net/wiki/index.php/Guthrie_Govan%2C_Book:_%22Creative_Guitar1:_Cutting_Edge_Techniques%22)

**Blues curricula**
- Berklee Online Blues Guitar sequence: E minor pentatonic and blues scales in open position, then the 6 minor pentatonic positions, then string bends (lesson 3), then A minor and C major pentatonic. — [Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/blues-guitar)
- Licklibrary "Electric Blues in 6 Weeks" sequence: Week 1 covers blues bends, string bending, vibrato, and bending combined with vibrato. Week 4 covers call and response, repetition, simplifying licks and "stretching the time". Week 5 covers major/minor call and response. — [Licklibrary](https://www.licklibrary.com/learn/courses/electric-blues-in-6-weeks-week-4)
- Box sequence: "The Beginner's Box, The Intermediate Box, The Albert King Box". — [Guitar Alliance](https://guitaralliance.net/?p=78372)
- Sliding from the minor 3rd to the major 3rd is "one of the most recognizable sounds in all of blues". Major pentatonic works over the I chord. — [TrueFire blog](https://blog.truefire.com/guitar-lessons/solo-over-the-blues/)
- An intro course runs from the 12-bar form, to pentatonic across the neck, to building a solo with dynamics, motifs and call and response. — [Til / Seth Rosenbloom](https://www.til.co/class/seth-rosenbloom-teaches-introduction-to-blues-guitar)

**Metal rhythm and prog**
- Odd meters: start with a 4/4 riff, then play it in 5/4, then 7/8 and 9/8. Use a metronome for polyrhythm work. — [Riffhard: Progressive Metal Guitar Lessons](https://www.riffhard.com/progressive-metal-guitar-lessons/)
- For cross-rhythms, learn the riff as its own odd meter, then break it into cells of 2, 3, 4 or 5 semiquavers. 7- and 8-string guitars are common in modern metal. — [Fundamental Changes](https://fundamental-changes.com/?p=5009)
- Licklibrary Metal Rhythm Workouts Vol. 2 includes 7/8 prog riffs (clean and distorted) and groups of three 16ths "common in progressive and djent". — [Licklibrary](https://www.licklibrary.com/learn/courses/metal-rhythm-guitar-workouts-vol-2)
- Downpicking routine (anecdotal): palm-muted downstrokes on low E at 80 bpm for 60 s on, 30 s off, cycling through 8ths, 8th triplets and 16ths, then +10 bpm once comfortable. Track strings with the palm rather than anchoring on the 6th string. — [Troy Grady forum: Fast Downstrokes for Thrash](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/fast-downstrokes-for-thrash-rhythms/985/104)
- There is a debate over stamina vs. coordination. One experienced member argues fatigue signals a technique problem and is not a lack of stamina. Others find minute-long relaxed endurance sets useful. — [Troy Grady forum](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/fast-downstrokes-for-thrash-rhythms/985?page=6)

### Inferences
- A practical taxonomy for the coach: Technique (fretting hand: legato, vibrato, bends, stretches; picking hand: alternate, down, economy/sweep, hybrid, tapping; tagged with Grady motion type), Rhythm (subdivisions, time feel, odd meters, polyrhythm, palm muting, gallops, tremolo), Theory/Fretboard (notes, CAGED/3nps, chord construction, modes, harmony), Ear, Repertoire, Gear/Tone, Improvisation and Composition.
- Picking-path design can use Grady's terms in its diagnostic questions, e.g. "Do you prefer to leave a string on a downstroke or an upstroke?" and "Which joint drives your motion?". Video self-recording is the diagnostic method Grady uses.

### Gaps
- I did not research Berklee or Musicians Institute degree progressions, Paul Gilbert's method (e.g. Intense Rock), or Rick Beato and Marty Music learning paths. These need a follow-up.
- I found no official chapter list for Govan's Creative Guitar 1 or official TOC for Rock Discipline. Both come from secondary sources.
- JustinGuitar Grades 4–9 module lists were not retrievable because the site returned 403.

## 3. Typical BPM benchmarks per level

### Takeaway
There is no authoritative standard. Forum consensus puts alternate-picked 16ths at about 120–160 bpm for intermediate players, about 180 bpm as "fast" (12 notes/s), and 200+ as shred. Single-string speeds run higher than string-changing speeds. Thrash downpicking targets are about 200–212 bpm 8ths (e.g. Master of Puppets), and the commonly quoted "220" is disputed.

### Cited Findings
- "Most people who want to pick fast are probably looking to get to 180 bpm at minimum." 16ths at 180 bpm equal 12 notes per second. — [Troy Grady forum: 3nps speeds](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/3-note-per-string-exercise/48586/3)
- A teacher's claim that 16ths at 160 were "pretty much the upper limit" was rebutted with Eric Johnson and metal players "way up over 200". — [jazzguitar.be forum](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/guitar-technique/50517-working-16th-notes-190bpm.html)
- Plateaus reported around 132–140 bpm 16ths and around 135 bpm. — [Troy Grady forum: speed past 135](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/need-help-increase-speed-past-135bpm/39597)
- One player reports 210 bpm 16ths on a single string, with cross-string scale patterns slower. Troy Grady and Martin Miller's clean 1nps playing is cited around 180–190 bpm 16ths. Estimates for Perpetual Burn are disputed. — [Troy Grady forum](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/is-it-possible-to-play-perpetual-burn-with-alternate-picking/39068)
- Master of Puppets is about 212 bpm according to one member, with tempo drift because there was no click. This disputes the "220" figure. — [Troy Grady forum: MoP riff](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/master-of-puppets-lick-what-strategies-would-you-use-to-get-this-up-to-tempo/9196)
- RSL technical-exercise tempos are 70 to 100 bpm from Debut to Grade 8 (see section 1). These are accuracy benchmarks, not speed benchmarks. — [RSL](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)

### Inferences
- Proposed speed tiers for alternate-picked 16ths, combining forum consensus with RSL tempos: Beginner under 90; Late beginner 90–110; Intermediate 110–140; Upper-intermediate 140–170; Advanced 170–200; Shred 200+. Each tier is split into single-string vs. string-crossing results.
- Proposed downpicked 8ths tiers: under 140 for rock/punk; 160–180 for typical thrash; 190–212+ for Metallica-tier work, sustained for more than 60 s.
- The coach should log "clean, relaxed, repeatable" tempo and not peak tempo. Forum users stress that fatigue signals a technique problem.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or institutional speed norms were found. All speed tiers are forum-derived.

## 4. Common plateaus/gaps of self-taught players and how teachers diagnose them

### Takeaway
Typical gaps are rhythm and timing (practising without a metronome), ear training, chord-tone-based improvisation (players stuck in pentatonic boxes), fretting-hand and posture habits (wrist angle, thumb wrapping, tension), barre-chord muting, and a "75% good enough" polish habit. Teachers diagnose by asking about background and goals, then listening to the student play for pitch, timing and rhythm and watching hand position.

### Cited Findings
- A first lesson starts with an evaluation of finger strength, hand position and placement, while listening for "pitch accuracy, timing, and rhythm". Teachers also ask about background and goals. — [Blayze blog](https://blayze.io/blog/guitar/how-to-avoid-bad-habits-as-you-learn-to-play-the-guitar)
- "Bad wrist angle, gripping the neck like a baseball bat, and tensing the shoulder are the three habits that quietly cap your progress for years... A teacher catches these in lesson one." "Self-taught guitarists almost universally underweight rhythm in the first year." — [Blayze blog](https://blayze.io/blog/guitar/how-to-avoid-bad-habits-as-you-learn-to-play-the-guitar)
- A teacher "will spot the wrist angle or barre-chord muting issue in fifteen minutes that you've been failing to fix for six months". — [musiciangoods](https://musiciangoods.com/blogs/guitar/can-you-learn-guitar-by-yourself)
- Plateaus often mean being strong in 1–2 of 5 core skills (fretboard, chords, theory, ear, rhythm) and weak in the rest. TrueFire's level descriptors run Beginner → Late beginner → Early intermediate ("the big middle... clear gaps") → Intermediate → Advanced ("strong command across all five skills"). — [TrueFire skill assessment](https://truefire.com/learn/guitar-skill-assessment-quiz?hsLang=en)
- Intermediates focus on scale boxes and neglect rhythm, "arguably the most important element". — [Licklibrary: 10 mistakes](https://www.licklibrary.com/news/10-common-mistakes-intermediate-guitarists-make)
- Practising without a metronome leads to weak rhythm. — [London Guitar Academy](https://www.londonguitaracademy.com/intermediate-guitar-player)
- Solos sound like scale patterns, with no improvisational concepts beyond pentatonics or chord-tone targeting. — [goodguitarist.com](https://goodguitarist.com/5-common-mistakes-self-taught-guitarists-make-and-how-to-fix-them)
- The "good enough" habit means stopping at about 75% polish. — [Just Play The Thing substack](https://justplaythething.substack.com/p/this-bad-habit-causes-self-taught)
- Thumb placement: "middle to the lower third of the back of the neck", not wrapped over the fretboard early on. — [thefretboard.co.uk](https://thefretboard.co.uk/discussion/293183/what-to-expect-or-ask-for-in-a-first-guitar-lesson)

### Inferences
- An AI coach cannot watch the player, so placement should combine self-report with quick objective micro-tests. Examples: play along to a click at set bpm and report whether you drift; name the notes on the A string at frets 3, 5, 7 and 8; record a bend to pitch and compare against a tuner.
- Vibrato quality and unwanted string noise are classic blind spots that self-report handles poorly. Ask the player to record and self-check against a tuner or with headphones.

### Gaps
- I found no sources specifically on vibrato as a diagnosed self-taught weakness, though it is widely assumed. No Reddit threads (r/guitarlessons, r/metalguitar) came up in search.

## 5. Draft placement questionnaire and path templates

### Takeaway
Everything in this section is a design proposal synthesized from sections 1–4. It is not sourced fact. It gives a 22-question placement interview mapped to the 8 taxonomy dimensions, 5-level descriptors and 5 path templates with milestones.

### Cited Findings
- The structural anchors come from the sources above: RSL exam components and time-learning descriptors ([RSL](https://www.rslawards.com/learn-electric-guitar/)); TrueFire's five-skill, five-level model ([TrueFire](https://truefire.com/learn/guitar-skill-assessment-quiz?hsLang=en)); Grady escape terminology ([troygrady.com](https://troygrady.com/?p=133543)); the downpicking interval routine ([Troy Grady forum](https://forum.troygrady.com/t/fast-downstrokes-for-thrash-rhythms/985/104)); the blues sequencing ([Berklee](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/blues-guitar), [Licklibrary](https://www.licklibrary.com/learn/courses/electric-blues-in-6-weeks-week-4)); and the odd-meter method ([Riffhard](https://www.riffhard.com/progressive-metal-guitar-lessons/), [Fundamental Changes](https://fundamental-changes.com/?p=5009)).

### Inferences
**Level descriptors (L1–L5, adapted from TrueFire and RSL):**
- L1 Beginner (about RSL Debut–G1, under 12 months): open chords and power chords, simple strumming, minor pentatonic box 1, keeps time at about 70 bpm.
- L2 Late beginner (about G2–G3, 1–2 years): barre chords, palm muting, alternate picking at 8ths, bends, 5 pentatonic boxes, 12-bar blues, 16ths at about 90–110.
- L3 Intermediate (about G4–G5): CAGED or 3nps across the neck, controlled vibrato and bends in tune, basic legato, full-song repertoire, 16ths at 110–140, downpicked 8ths at 160+.
- L4 Upper-intermediate (about G6–G7): modes and chord-tone soloing, sweep and tapping basics, 7/8 and 5/4 riffs, 16ths at 140–170.
- L5 Advanced (about G8+): stylistic fluency, odd groupings and polyrhythms, 16ths at 170+, composes and arranges.

**Placement questionnaire (22 questions):**
1. How long have you been playing, and how many hours per week right now? (context, maps to RSL time bands)
2. Self-taught, teacher, or online course? Which courses (JustinGuitar grade, RSL grade, etc.)? (context)
3. Which genres and artists do you most want to sound like? (goals)
4. What's your #1 goal for the next 3 months: a specific song, speed, soloing, writing, or a band/gig? (goals)
5. Name 3 songs you can play start-to-finish at full tempo, and one you're stuck on. (repertoire)
6. Can you play full songs along with the record without stopping? (repertoire/rhythm)
7. Do you practise with a metronome or drum track? Highest bpm for clean alternate-picked 16ths: on one string, and across strings? (technique/rhythm)
8. Highest bpm for continuous downpicked palm-muted 8ths for 60 seconds, without tension? (metal rhythm)
9. Which of these can you play cleanly: barre chords, palm-muted chugs, gallops, tremolo picking, string skipping, legato 3nps, sweep arpeggios (3/5 string), tapping, hybrid picking? (technique inventory)
10. When you change strings during fast picking, which stroke feels natural for leaving a string (down or up)? Does the motion come from the wrist, elbow or forearm? (Grady diagnosis)
11. Bends: can you bend a whole step in tune (checked with a tuner) with the 3rd finger? (expression)
12. Vibrato: is it controlled in width and speed, and can you vary it? Is it wrist or finger driven? (expression)
13. When you play with gain, do you get unwanted string noise? How do you mute (palm, fretting-hand fingers, thumb)? (muting)
14. Can you name any note on the low E and A strings within about 2 seconds? (fretboard)
15. Which scale systems do you know: pentatonic boxes 1–5, CAGED, 3nps, modes? (fretboard/theory)
16. Can you explain what makes a chord major, minor, dominant 7 or diminished, and find the key of a song? (theory)
17. Can you play a 12-bar blues in any key and solo over it, switching between minor and major pentatonic? (blues/improv)
18. When soloing, do you target chord tones, or mainly run scale patterns? (improvisation)
19. Can you work out a simple riff or melody by ear? How long does it take? (ear)
20. Have you played anything in 5/4, 7/8 or with groupings of 3 over 4? (prog rhythm)
21. Gear: guitar, tuning and string count (6/7/8), amp or modeller. Can you dial a tight high-gain tone versus a blues crunch? (gear/tone)
22. Have you written your own riffs or songs? Do you record yourself? (composition/self-review)

The coach can follow up with micro-tests, e.g. "play this at X bpm to the click and record it". Answers set the starting level for each taxonomy dimension separately, not one global level.

**Per-path deep-dive questions (examples):**
- Downpicking: current clean tempo at 30 s and 60 s; where tension appears (forearm, shoulder); whether the palm anchors or tracks; whether string-crossing riffs (e.g. Master of Puppets, Battery) are the target.
- Blues phrasing: favourite players; whether bends land in pitch; whether vibrato is used on every long note; ability to leave space; call-and-response comfort.
- Prog rhythm: can you count 7/8 as 2+2+3; do you lose the downbeat; 7- or 8-string; target bands (Dream Theater, Periphery, Meshuggah).
- Learn a song: song, tuning, the hardest section, current tempo vs. target tempo, and whether lead or rhythm.

**Path templates with milestones:**
1. **Downpicking stamina to 200 bpm 8ths** (L2–L4, about 8–16 weeks). Milestone 0: baseline the clean 60 s tempo. M1: 80 bpm interval sets (60 s on, 30 s off) in 8ths, triplets and 16ths, +10 bpm when relaxed. M2: 160 bpm for 60 s with palm tracking across strings. M3: a string-crossing riff at 180. M4: 200 bpm for 60 s, then full songs (Master of Puppets at about 212) end to end. Log tension self-rating each session.
2. **Blues soloing phrasing** (L2–L3, about 8 weeks). W1: 12-bar form and shuffle rhythm. W2: minor pentatonic box 1 plus the "Albert King box". W3: bends to pitch (half, whole, pre-bend) checked with a tuner. W4: vibrato control. W5: call and response, repetition, space. W6: minor-to-major 3rd slide and major pentatonic over the I chord. W7: link boxes across the neck. W8: record a 3-chorus solo with dynamics and motifs.
3. **Odd meters prog rhythm** (L3–L4, about 6–10 weeks). M1: count and clap 5/4, 7/8 and 9/8. M2: re-meter a known 4/4 riff into 5/4 and 7/8. M3: 16th-note groupings of 3 over 4/4 (djent cell). M4: cells of 2/3/4/5 to build a riff. M5: learn a reference song section in 7/8. M6: write an original 8-bar odd-meter riff.
4. **Alternate picking speed, 16ths** (L2–L5). Diagnose motion type (USX/DSX/DBX) from a self-recorded close-up. Single-string bursts, then 2-string patterns that leave on the escape stroke, then scale fragments (Petrucci-style). Tiers at 110, 140, 170 and 200. Log clean and relaxed tempo separately from peak tempo.
